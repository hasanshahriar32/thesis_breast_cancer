Thesis foundation

The clinical data-sharing constraint is the real problem.

01

Histopathology benefits from multi-site evidence

Robust breast-cancer classifiers need diverse images and patient populations, while each hospital naturally holds only a partial view of the problem.

Raw image centralization creates governance friction

Medical images are sensitive. Collaborative training must respect institutional control, privacy obligations, and practical limitations on data sharing.

Federated learning is the methodological response

Hospitals retain local data while the server aggregates locally computed model updates to create a shared model.

Thesis scope

Privacy-preserving breast-cancer image classification across heterogeneous hospital data, with FedAvg as the baseline and FedProx as a tested stabilization method.

Sources: McMahan et al. (2017); Rehman et al. (2023).
